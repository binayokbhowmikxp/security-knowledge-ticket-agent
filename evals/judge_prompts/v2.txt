RESPONSE-TYPE ASSESSMENT — apply this before evidence and citation checks.
Identify the response's primary type from its actual content and purpose, not merely the presence of an asset ID or a keyword:
1. Evidence-based investigation: reports or compares source-specific inventory, documentation or historical findings.
2. Clarification or missing-information response: asks for identifiers or sanitized evidence needed to proceed, or honestly reports a failed inventory lookup.
3. Security refusal: declines unsafe or unauthorized activity and directs the requester to an approved process.
4. General procedural guidance: explains technical checks, operational steps or PAM knowledge, even without a named asset.
5. Out-of-scope response: declines requests beyond the assistant's supported PAM scope.
Briefly identify the type in the concise reasoning, then evaluate the evidence requirements appropriate to the actual claims. Keep the existing reasoning/label output schema; do not add a response-type output field.

Clarifications and security refusals CAN be good without retrieval or citations if they make no unsupported source-specific factual claims or documented technical procedures. Merely repeating the asset identifier or user-reported symptom while requesting clarification or refusing a prohibited action is not a verified inventory claim. A concise boundary statement or request to consult an authorized owner need not retrieve an asset or historical ticket to justify the refusal. An appropriate out-of-scope refusal likewise does not need irrelevant PAM retrieval.

An honest asset_not_found response, supported by the saved failed lookup, must not cite a nonexistent record. Do not demand inventory details that are unavailable or treat absence of a record as inadequate completeness when the response accurately explains the limit and requests approved identifying information. If no asset can be identified, requesting an ID/account identifier can be sufficient; do not require the model to guess an inventory record.

Do not use these exceptions to excuse unsupported facts or substantive technical procedures hidden inside a clarification or refusal. For a mixed response, assess each component: source-specific assertions and documented troubleshooting steps still require the appropriate tools, relevant retrieved evidence and nearby correctly formatted citations. Distinguish basic requests for sanitized errors/identifiers from instructions to inspect or change technical configuration. A response's self-description as a refusal or clarification is not an exemption. Do not force an investigation to pass as a clarification when necessary available evidence was genuinely omitted.
